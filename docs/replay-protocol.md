# Replay handoff protocol

How a dissection "published" by the research desk's replay reaches the mobile app. One page;
the desk and the app both implement against this file, so a drift here is a drift everywhere.

## The replay itself

Data: `content/replay.json`, generated by `pnpm build:replay` from the committed run log.
Nothing in it is invented; see the file's own `disclosure` strings, which the console MUST keep
visible for the whole replay (blueprint 3.4 honesty of simulation). No durations are carried:
the console paces events theatrically and the disclosure says the pacing is compressed and not
to scale.

## Channel: same machine, automatic

- BroadcastChannel name: `mth-updates`
- Message shape: `{ "type": "published", "narrative_id": "<id>", "at": "<ISO timestamp>" }`
- Sender: the research desk, once, when a replay reaches its `published` event.
- Receiver: the app shell (`/app`, and any mounted `App` at `/n/{id}` or `/share`). On receipt:
  show the update toast, mark the narrative with the just-updated badge, persist.

## Channel: separate device, by QR

- The `published` panel renders a QR encoding `${SITE_URL}/n/{narrative_id}?published=1`
  (`SITE_URL` from `app/src/site.ts`, the real production origin).
- The mobile side treats `?published=1` exactly as a received broadcast: badge, toast, persist.
  A static host cannot push across devices without a server, and this product deliberately has
  no server; the QR IS the cross-device channel, and a judge scanning it on their own phone is
  the demo.

## Persistence

- localStorage key: `mth:updated` holding `{ [narrative_id]: "<ISO timestamp>" }`.
- The badge renders wherever the narrative appears (feed card, autopsy provenance line) until
  the reader opens that autopsy, which clears that id from the map.
- For the `via_dissect` narrative (`ppn-panic`), a publish event ALSO flips the same feed-join
  flag the fresh-dissect demo flips, through the same code path. One mechanism, two doors.

## QR encoder decision (blueprint 7.5 justification)

`uqr` (MIT, zero dependencies, pure TypeScript, ~4 KB gzip, tree-shakeable `renderSVG`).
Why a dependency at all: a QR encoder is Reed-Solomon coding plus format/version tables, a
class of code that is all edge cases and no product value; hand-rolling it is rung 7 of the
ladder when rung 5 exists. Why `uqr` over `qrcode`: no Node canvas dependency, no transitive
tree, SVG output that inherits the surface's ink color. Pinned exact, lockfile committed.
Loaded lazily by the research desk only, so no other route pays for it.
